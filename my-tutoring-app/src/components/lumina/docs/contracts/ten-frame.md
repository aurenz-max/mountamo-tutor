### R2 — frame capacity and grade band remain coherent · OBSERVED · **FORKED 2026-09-08**

- **Property:** K uses a single 10-cell frame; Grades 1–2 may use a double frame for values through 20. `make_ten` is specifically complement-to-10 and remains pinned to a single frame at every grade; a future make-20 task requires a separate eval mode.
- **Demanded by:** catalog constraints, grade fidelity, TF-3 product decision.
- **Evidence:** generator grade/mode validation and post-config make-ten pin; EVAL_TRACKER TF-3.
- **Probe:** make-ten eval output uses `mode: 'single'` and a target below 10.
- **The K single-frame pin now has exactly two named exemptions, and they are MODES, not a widened band.** `build_teen` and `decompose_teen` are pinned to a DOUBLE frame at every grade, K included — the mirror image of the single-frame pins `make_ten` and `split` carry, and for the same kind of reason: a ten cannot be a UNIT on a frame that holds exactly ten, so K.NBT.1's "ten ones and some further ones" has no picture on a single frame. Every other mode still collapses to a single frame at K, unchanged. The generator enforces both directions after config overrides (`hasTeenTypes → mode = 'double'`), and a teen item that reaches a single-frame session is DROPPED on both sides of the wire rather than truncated — which is precisely the defect the K Math atlas found and this fork closes (`ten-frame / build` emitting a single frame with a target of 5 for an objective naming 11-19).
- **Probe (teen):** a pinned `build_teen` or `decompose_teen` draw returns `mode: 'double'` with every `targetCount` in 11-19; `itemFromChallenge` returns null for a teen challenge at capacity 10. `__tests__/TenFrame.di-script.test.ts` §10.

### R4 — subitize is flash-then-hide recognition, not tap-counting · OBSERVED

- **Property:** Counters appear for `flashDuration`, then hide before the answer surface becomes available. The child can request another flash; hidden counters cannot be manipulated. A correct response restores the counters.
- **Demanded by:** `subitize` task identity and perceptual-fluency pedagogy.
- **Evidence:** component `startFlash`, hidden-phase guard in `handleCellClick`, and the flash lifecycle; generator grade/tier flash windows.
- **Probe:** subitize eval-test PASS; component timing path hides counters before the tutor's ask can be answered.

"A correct response restores the counters" now hangs off the tutor's AFFIRM (`onAffirmed`), not a Check click — the button it used to hang off no longer exists. "Show again" re-shows the stimulus and the paired tap-to-hear re-asks the QUESTION; neither may narrate the count.

### R5 — make-ten is derived from one numeric source of truth · OBSERVED

- **Demanded by:** TF-3/SP-17, answer-key consistency, pedagogy rule #1.
- **Evidence:** generator validation + `buildInstruction`; `qa/eval-reports/ten-frame-2026-05-28.md`; ten-frame oracle.

### R6 — answer surface forks by band without changing task identity · REQUIRED

- **Property:**
  - **`make_ten` @ K:** DIRECT MANIPULATION — seed `targetCount` counters; the child taps empty cells to fill the frame; the number of counters the child placed (`filledCount - targetCount`) is the enacted complement and auto-judges when the frame reaches 10. Initial counters are not removable. No make-ten stepper and no Check button.
  - **`make_ten` @ Grades 1–2:** the numeric complement stays the child's own unaided answer. **Re-based 2026-08-13:** it is SPOKEN and judged in-band; the stepper and Check button are gone. The stepper was a costume (a child who cannot find the complement can still operate one); the production demand is unchanged, which is why the eval mode's β is unchanged.
  - **`build` at every band:** DIRECT MANIPULATION, same re-basing as K make-ten — placing counters IS building the quantity, so the surface survives and the tutor judges the committed placement.
  - **`subitize`, `add`, `subtract` at every band:** the answer is the NUMBER, now spoken. Their working surfaces are unchanged.
  - **Challenge transitions:** every challenge owns its starting frame state. `add` starts empty; `make_ten` seeds `targetCount`; `subtract` seeds `startCount`; no completed frame carries into the next challenge.
- **Demanded by:** reader-fit item 12; direct-manipulation-first ruling for K act/build scenes; qa/di/BACKLOG.md item 18.
- **Evidence:** `qa/HANDOFF-direct-manipulation-fixes-2026-07-16.md`; reader-fit BACKLOG item 12; `qa/HANDOFF-di-ten-frame-2026-08-12.md`.
- **Probe:** `__tests__/TenFrame.reader-fit.test.tsx` — taps change the enacted K answer and it commits only when the frame is full; no stepper and no Check control at any band; a completed make-ten → add transition starts with zero counters; a Grade 1–2 make-ten item classifies as `answerKind: 'voice'` and its taps commit nothing.
- **Judged-loop addition (does not weaken the above):** a hands-only turn also closes on STILLNESS (`PLACEMENT_SETTLE_MS`), the gesture analogue of the mic's silence bracket. This is what makes a hand item JUDGEABLE — stopping before the frame is full is now a wrong answer the tutor corrects, where previously the only reachable state was correct. R6's frame-full auto-judge is untouched.

### R9 — `split` partitions a GIVEN group, and a session asks for more than one way · REQUIRED

- **Property:**
  - **The group arrives.** A `split` item seeds the frame with `targetCount` counters, all in `SPLIT_COLOR_A` (red). The child does not build it — `build` is the mode that does that, and conflating the two is exactly what the lesson-coverage judge caught ("only asks to place N counters on a frame").
  - **Taps FLIP, never add or remove.** A tap on a seeded counter toggles it between red and yellow; a tap on an empty cell does nothing. The total therefore cannot drift, and the only variable the item measures is where the line between the two groups falls. This is the two-colour counter in software — one disc, red on one face, yellow on the other — and it is what `showOptions.allowFlip` was always describing.
  - **The colours are PINNED** to red/yellow (`SPLIT_COLOR_A`/`SPLIT_COLOR_B`), because every scripted line names them. `twoColorMode`'s generator-chosen palette governs the decorative build challenges it was written for and does not reach `split`; a palette that could disagree with the tutor's mouth is the drift SP-17 removed from instruction text.
  - **Any two non-empty groups are correct.** There is no single right answer, which is what makes this a decomposition task rather than an arithmetic one. The signature miss is the EMPTY PART — flipping all or none — a fluent, finished-looking action that yields one group and nothing.
  - **A REPEAT is wrong while another way remains.** "Decompose in more than one way" (K.OA.3) is not assessable from any single item however well judged, so the session is the unit: the component keeps a per-total ledger of the pairs the child has produced and a later item on the same total that repeats one is corrected. Pairs are ORDERED (3+2 and 2+3 are different pictures and different bonds), giving a total of N exactly N−1 ways. Once a total's ways are exhausted the rule stands down — an unwinnable item is worse than a repeat.
  - **`splitOrdinal` has ONE producer,** `itemsFromChallenges`, so the stage and the headless drive plan ask the same question. Ordinal 1 asks for "a way"; every later one asks for "a DIFFERENT way".
  - **Commits on STILLNESS,** like `build`, with no terminal state and no commit control; a wrong partition commits exactly as readily as a right one.
  - **THE LEAK HERE IS A PAIR, NOT A COUNT.** The TOTAL is public — the ask states it. No ask, how-to-play, re-ask, correction or context push may name a part, and the ban holds for the whole session, because one valid pair spoken aloud answers every remaining item on that total. The pair may appear in exactly two places: the tutor's affirmation of what the child built, and the on-screen reveal after she affirms. There is no count readout on a split item — the honest one would be the parts.
- **Demanded by:** `qa/HANDOFF-decompose-to-5-counter-split-2026-09-06.md` (routed from `qa/lesson-bench/BACKLOG.md` item 29(a)); CCSS K.OA.3; two independent lesson-coverage draws scoring obj1 `TAUGHT_NOT_ASSESSED` / `ASSESSED_INDIRECTLY`.
- **Evidence:** `tenFrameScript.ts` (`judgeSplit`, `splitVerdictCue`, `splitCorrectionFor`, `itemsFromChallenges`); `TenFrame.tsx` (`flippedCells`, `shownSplitsRef`, the split branch of `handleCellClick`/`commitPlacement`); generator `split` docs + gate + paired fallback; catalog `decompose` eval mode; backend `PROBLEM_TYPE_REGISTRY["ten-frame"]["decompose"]` β 2.0.
- **Probe:** `__tests__/TenFrame.di-script.test.ts` §9 (15 cases: the drop gate, per-total ordinals, the three verdicts, exhaustion, the pair-leak scan, sentinel collisions, the gesture channel); `__tests__/TenFrame.reader-fit.test.tsx` (the stage: inert empty cells, flip and un-flip, stillness commit, a wrong partition committing, the ledger surviving an advance, the correction reset, no count readout, reveal only after affirm); `scripts/probe-ten-frame-split.mjs` — 3/3 live draws on the handoff's own topic.

### R10 — the teen modes show a TEN as one thing, and never hand it over · REQUIRED

- **Property:**
  - **The frame is double at every band** (R2's fork above). `build_teen` opens with the TOP frame full — ten counters — and the child taps the empty boxes below it until the two frames hold the teen number the tutor asked for; the counters they place are the ones, and the seeded ten is not removable. `decompose_teen` opens with the whole teen group already on the board, all red, and the child turns exactly ten of them yellow.
  - **`decompose_teen`'s group is SCATTERED, and that is pedagogy, not decoration.** Seeded top-frame-first, "flip the full row" would be a layout cue a child who cannot count to ten could follow — the trivially-solvable-from-layout failure pedagogy rule #1 forbids. The scatter is produced once, in `itemsFromChallenges` (the same one-producer rule `splitOrdinal` follows), deterministic in the item id so a re-render cannot move counters out from under a counting finger, and re-rolled off the same stream until neither frame is full. **At nineteen this is geometrically impossible** — nineteen counters over two frames of ten must fill one — so that one total degrades to "spot the full frame". It is kept because the published objective names 16-19, and a cap below what the objective names is the defect this mode closes.
  - **`build_teen` closes on STILLNESS and must never auto-commit on a full frame.** K make-ten judges the moment the frame fills (R6) because filling it IS its answer; here filling all twenty is a WRONG answer to "make fourteen", so a frame-driven commit would make the frame the judge. The component reads `commitAt` directly rather than falling back to `capacity`, which is what keeps the two apart.
  - **Both are judged in CODE against a target the mode fixes** — the ones on `build_teen`, ten on `decompose_teen` — and the tutor is handed a verdict, never a board.
  - **THE LEAK HERE IS THE LEFTOVER.** The teen number and the ten are both PUBLIC: the ask states the total, and "a full frame is ten" is the model K.NBT.1 exists to teach. What may never precede the child's answer is how many are left over. That ban holds across the ask, the how-to-play, the re-ask, the context push, the printed instruction, and — because a live draw was caught doing it — the generator's `hint` and `narration`, which are code-owned on these two modes for the same reason `instruction` already was. `decompose_teen`'s correction never names the leftover at all: it is byte-identical across totals, which is the property that proves it carries no information about the answer.
  - **The teen numbers themselves are code-owned.** `teenWindow.ts` reads the window out of the lesson's own words and sweeps it; a live draw against "Break apart numbers 16-19" had come back 13, 14, 14, 15, 15, 16 before this.
- **Demanded by:** CCSS K.NBT.1; the K Math coverage atlas rows COUNT001-05-A..F and COUNT001-03-G, which had no working home before this.
- **Evidence:** `tenFrameScript.ts` (`judgeTeen`, `teenVerdictCue`, `teenCorrectionFor`, `scatterCells`, the teen build gates); `TenFrame.tsx` (the seeded-ten branch, the flip branch, `commitAt`); `gemini-ten-frame.ts` (the double-frame pin, the askable gate, the code-owned sweep and prose); `teenWindow.ts`; catalog `build_teen` / `decompose_teen`; backend `PROBLEM_TYPE_REGISTRY["ten-frame"]` β 2.0 / 3.0.
- **Probe:** `__tests__/TenFrame.di-script.test.ts` §10 (13 cases: the double-frame pin and the single-frame drop, the 11-19 window, the two boards, the scatter guard, the leak scans, the code judge and its forked corrections, the invariance proof, the shared gesture entry point, the harness's signature misses, the action change); `__tests__/TenFrame.reader-fit.test.tsx` (the stage: twenty cells at K, the fixed ten, no auto-commit on a full frame, flip-only taps with inert empty cells, the stillness commit, wrong commits, no count readout on `decompose_teen`, the scatter surviving a correction); live eval-test draws on the atlas's own objectives, 18/18 and 16/16 content checks over two draws each.

## Conflicts

### C1 — R2 (K is a single frame) vs K.NBT.1 (a ten and some ones) — **RESOLVED via rung 1, eval-mode split, 2026-09-08**

Both demands are right for their consumers and they cannot both hold on one frame. R2's single-frame pin at K was written for counting, subitizing and make-ten, where ten is the CEILING and a second frame is twenty empty boxes around a number the child is not working with. K.NBT.1 needs ten to be a UNIT with room beside it, which a ten-cell frame cannot show. An in-place edit — "allow the double frame at K when the objective names 11-19", which is what the atlas's own next-action proposed — would have put every K counting lesson one manifest misroute away from a twenty-cell board, ablating exactly the consumers R2 protects.

So the fork took the cheapest rung: two new eval modes, pinned to a double frame at every band, with every existing mode's single-frame pin untouched and enforced after config overrides in both directions. R1 extends by two rows. R6's fork table gains two gesture rows that hold at BOTH bands (like `split`'s, unlike make-ten's) — a reader who can say "ten and four" has still not shown they can build it. R3, R4, R5, R7, R8 and R9 are untouched: nothing that reaches `build`, `subitize`, `make_ten`, `split`, `add` or `subtract` changed, which the pack's other 47 cases and the stage's other 22 re-verified after the edit.

_No other conflict open._ R9 is **COMPATIBLE / new mode**, and deliberately so: the handoff's own reading found that `counting-board` and `ten-frame` had no split mechanic and that `twoColorMode` — which looks like one — is a static POSITIONAL pre-colouring with no per-counter choice behind it. A new challenge type plus a new eval mode was therefore the fork-on-conflict answer, not an edit to `build`: `build`'s consumers ask for a QUANTITY and would have been ablated by a mode that also demands a partition. R1's "generated challenge type follows the selected eval mode" extends by one row; R2 gains a second single-frame pin (`split`, for the same reason `make_ten` has one); R6's fork table gains a gesture row that holds at BOTH bands, unlike make-ten's — a reader who can say "two and three" has still not shown they can partition a set. R3, R4, R5, R7 and R8 are untouched.

Item 12 is **COMPATIBLE / fork-by-band+mode**. It changes only R6's K `make_ten` answer surface. R4 requires subitize to keep its hidden numeric response; R3 requires build/count-all to keep its construction-plus-check behavior; the non-K branch of R6 preserves the established Grade 1–2 response. R5's numeric source of truth already contains everything needed, so no generator schema change is justified.
